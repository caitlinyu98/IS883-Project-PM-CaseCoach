# Rubrics

## metrics
A strong metrics answer starts with the goal of the product before naming any number. It picks one north-star metric that reflects the value customers get, then adds two or three supporting metrics and one guardrail metric that would warn if the team optimizes the wrong thing. In B2B, it separates the buyer who pays from the user who works in the product every day, because they care about different outcomes. It says how each metric is measured and over what time period, and it explains why it rejected at least one obvious metric.

## no_success_metrics
Signs: the candidate describes a solution in detail but never says how anyone would know it worked. They may say "users will love it" or "it will improve engagement" without naming a number. How to fix: name one metric tied to the goal, say how it is measured, and give a target or a direction, for example "% of managers who log waste weekly, measured over the first 60 days."
